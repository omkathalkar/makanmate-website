How Language Models Work

A from-scratch guide, built with code

I'm a PhD student working on efficient AI inference. Over the past couple of years I've read a lot of papers, stared at a lot of code, and spent an embarrassing amount of time confused about things that turned out to be simple once someone explained them properly.

This wiki is my attempt to write the explanations I wish I'd had. Not the textbook version. Not the "here's the formal definition" version. The version where we build a tiny working thing first, stare at it until it makes sense, and then talk about how real systems scale it up.

I've tried to follow one rule throughout: intuition first, math and code second. If the code still feels magical after reading the explanation, the explanation isn't good enough yet. I'll keep fixing it.

Prerequisites: basic Python, a rough idea of what a neural network is (weights, forward pass, loss, training). You don't need to know PyTorch — I'll explain what I use as I go. No math beyond high-school algebra is assumed, though some familiarity with matrix multiplication will help.
Topics
  • 1. Tokenization
    Before a model can touch your text, it converts it to integers. How that works, why it's trickier than it sounds, and why "1+1=2" is five tokens not three.
  • 2. Embeddings
    Token integers get mapped to vectors — points in a high-dimensional space where meaning lives. How the geometry of that space actually encodes relationships, and where the intuition breaks down.
  • 3. Attention
    The core mechanism of every modern LLM. Every token looks at every other token and decides what to borrow. We build scaled dot-product attention from 20 lines of PyTorch.
  • 4. The Transformer Block
    Attention + layer norm + feedforward network, wrapped in residual connections. Why this particular combination works so well, and what the full GPT architecture looks like.
  • 5. Training & Backprop
    How the model learns from data. Cross-entropy loss, gradient descent, AdamW. What loss curves actually tell you — and what they don't.
  • 6. Inference
    What happens when you hit send. Autoregressive generation, KV caching (the most important optimization you've never heard of), and the sampling strategies that control how "creative" the model is.
